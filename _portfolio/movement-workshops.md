Since 2024, I design spaces where play is taken seriously.
My movement workshop practice sits at the intersection of improvisation theater, movement, and participatory facilitation. 
Through Playing Silly and Queering Improv, I create between 90-minute and 2 hours sessions where people, especially marginalized folx, can reconnect with their bodies, their weirdness, and each other.
Participants explore how to make others shine, question gender roles and power dynamics through scenes, practice consent in real time, and include boundaries expressed with kindness. I make room for neurodivergence, softness, for whatever is messy, nonlinear, or hard to label.
I'm particularly interested in what happens when queer feminist values shape the structure of play itself: who gets to lead, who gets to take up space, what counts as a valid contribution. The exercises I use draw from theater games, physical theater, body-based awareness practices, and collective improvisation, adapted for people with no prior experience and no interest in performing for anyone.
